# Evidence ladder

Grade every piece of evidence for an assumption from 0 to 5. The grade is about **how directly the evidence shows real behaviour by the real audience in a realistic situation** — not about how official the source sounds.

| Grade | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Belief** | The team thinks so. No outside evidence. | "Everyone we know would use this." Internal enthusiasm. AI persona reactions. |
| **1 — Opinion** | People say so, when asked. | Survey answers about hypothetical use, interview statements ("I'd definitely use that"), likes and comments. |
| **2 — Analogy** | Real behaviour, but in a different context. | A comparable scheme elsewhere, a different audience, a different country, national statistics that describe the situation but not the response to your idea. |
| **3 — Small commitment** | The real audience takes a real but low-cost action towards your idea. | Clicks through a fake-door page, joins a waiting list, books a slot, completes a task in a usability test, gives an email address. |
| **4 — Real commitment** | The real audience gives up something that costs them. | Pays, pre-orders, turns up in person, gives significant time, uses it a second time unprompted. |
| **5 — Sustained behaviour** | Repeated real use over time, at the scale the idea needs. | Retention after three months, renewals, usage at target volumes in a pilot. |

## How to use it

- **Grade the evidence, not the assumption.** An assumption can have several pieces of evidence at different grades. Record the highest *relevant* grade, and note conflicting evidence.
- **Context lowers the grade.** Evidence from a different audience or place is at most a 2, even if it's behavioural.
- **Size matters, but differently at each rung.** Ten people paying is stronger than a thousand people saying they would. But ten people can't tell you what share of a population will behave that way — only that some will.
- **Opinion is useful for *why*, not *whether*.** Interviews are the best tool for understanding reasons, language and barriers. They're poor at predicting uptake. Use them for the first and not the second.
- **Synthetic personas are grade 0.** They're a way to rehearse questions, not a way to learn about people.

## Why "said" ranks below "did"

What people say they'll do reliably overstates what they end up doing, particularly for choices seen as virtuous — sustainable, ethical, healthy. This is well documented as the "intention–behaviour gap". A 2025 study of climate action found intentions explained only around a fifth to a quarter of the variation in what people actually did ([Dablander et al., PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280983/)). Research on sharing schemes specifically found that people's positive attitudes towards sharing didn't necessarily turn into taking part, and that saving money predicted participation better than environmental values did ([Hamari et al., 2016](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/the-sharing-economy-why-people-participate-in-collaborative-consu)).

Treat any grade-1 evidence for a pro-social or pro-environmental idea as an upper bound.
